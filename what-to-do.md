# What To Do If You Suspect an Incident

## Step 1: Stop What You are Doing

Do not continue **using the affected device or system.** If you clicked a link or opened an attachment, **disconnect from the network** (unplug Ethernet, turn off Wi-Fi) but **do not power off the device**.

**Warning!** Powering off the device can destroy evidence the security team needs.

## Step 2: Do Not Delete Anything

Do not delete the suspicious email, file, or message. Do not empty your trash or recycle bin. Do not run antivirus scans on your own.

## Step 3: Report Immediately

| Method | Contact | Use For |
|---|---|---|
| **IT Security Hotline** | [Phone Number] | Urgent incidents (ransomware, active breach) |
| **IT Security Email** | Non-urgent reports, phishing emails |
| **IT Help Desk** | [Phone Number] / [Email] | Device issues, access problems |
| **Compliance Hotline** | [Phone Number] | PHI exposure, privacy concerns |
| **Your Manager** | Direct contact | All incidents (keep them informed) |

## Step 4: Provide the Following Information

1. Your name and department
2. What happened (in plain language)
3. When it happened (date and time)
4. What system, device, or data was involved
5. What you have already done (e.g., disconnected from network)
6. Who else may be affected
7. Screenshots (if safe to take do not click anything else)

## Step 5: Preserve Evidence

Do not delete, move, or alter anything. Leave the device, email, or file exactly as it is.

## Step 6: Wait for Instructions

The IT Security team will contact you with next steps. Do not attempt to fix the issue yourself.

## Step 7: Cooperate with the Investigation

You may be asked to provide a written statement, meet with the security team, hand over your device for forensic review, or identify others affected.

**Note** Reporting an incident in good faith will never result in punishment.
